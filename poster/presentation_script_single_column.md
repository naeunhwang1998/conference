# 3-minute oral presentation script: single-column ePoster

**Clinical review of robotic distal pancreatectomy**, IHPBA 2026
About 420 words, roughly 3 minutes at a calm pace (~140 words per minute). *[Brackets]* show where to point on the poster.

---

**Opening (0:00–0:15)** *[Title and logos]*
Good morning, everyone. I'm Naeun Hwang from the Upper GI Unit at Health New Zealand, Waitematā, and the University of Auckland. I'll present our clinical review of robotic distal pancreatectomy, or RDP.

**The headline (0:15–0:35)** *[Four numbers across the top]*
I'll start with the four numbers at the top, because they sum up the review. Compared with laparoscopic surgery, RDP gives about a one-day shorter hospital stay, zero percent positive margins against two percent, and an overall cost that is no higher. The trade-off, shown in gold, is about thirty minutes of extra operating time.

**01 Background and aim (0:35–0:55)**
Distal pancreatectomy has moved from open surgery to laparoscopy, and now to the robot. Laparoscopy works well but is limited by dexterity and visualisation, and whether the robot actually improves outcomes is still debated. So we compared robotic with open and laparoscopic distal pancreatectomy across technique, outcomes, cost and challenges.

**02 Why robotic? (0:55–1:10)**
The robot offers 3D magnified vision, tremor filtration, motion scaling and wristed instruments. Almost all the evidence comes from the da Vinci platform. Newer systems such as Versius and hinotori have only case reports so far.

**03 Operative technique (1:10–1:30)** *[Fig. 1, steps 1–5]*
Port placement is shown in Figure 1. The five key steps are positioning in reverse Trendelenburg and docking, entering the lesser sac, controlling the splenic artery and vein, dividing the pancreas with a clear margin, and completing an en bloc resection. The spleen can be preserved by saving the splenic vessels or with the Warshaw technique.

**04 Clinical outcomes (1:30–2:10)** *[Table: green, grey, amber]*
In this table, green favours RDP. Against open surgery, RDP has less blood loss, fewer complications, a shorter stay and more spleen preservation. Against laparoscopy, overall complication and fistula rates are similar. However, RDP has fewer conversions to open surgery, more spleen preservation, a shorter stay and better oncological results, with more lymph nodes retrieved. The one amber cell is operating time, which is longer mainly because of docking.

**05 Cost-effectiveness (2:10–2:30)** *[Three cost tiles]*
Theatre costs are higher with the robot. However, the overall cost is similar to laparoscopy and lower than open surgery, because the shorter stay and fewer complications offset it. RDP also gives more quality-adjusted life years than laparoscopy at a similar cost.

**06 Challenges (2:30–2:45)**
The main barriers are docking time, few training centres, and a high upfront cost that leads to unequal access to robotic surgery.

**Conclusion (2:45–3:00)** *[Navy conclusion box, QR code]*
To conclude, RDP offers greater precision, better outcomes and more spleen preservation than open and laparoscopic surgery, and it is cost-effective. Wider adoption will need training and equitable access. The QR code links to the full paper. Thank you.

## If you need to adjust timing

## Likely questions (short answers)
- **Is this a systematic review?** "No, it's a clinical (narrative) review of the published comparative studies, meta-analyses and cost analyses."
- **Where does the extra 30 minutes come from?** "Mostly robot docking and set-up, and it shortens along the learning curve."
- **What about pancreatic fistula?** "Rates are similar between robotic and laparoscopic, and lower than open."
