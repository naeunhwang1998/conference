**Clinical review of robotic distal pancreatectomy**, IHPBA 2026

**Opening (0:00–0:12)** *[Title and logos]*
Good morning. I'm Naeun Hwang from Health New Zealand, Waitematā, and the University of Auckland, presenting our clinical review of robotic distal pancreatectomy, or RDP.

**The headline (0:12–0:32)** *[Four numbers across the top]*
These four numbers sum up the review. Compared with laparoscopy, RDP gives about a one-day shorter stay, zero percent positive margins against two percent, and no higher overall cost. The trade-off, in gold, is about thirty minutes of extra operating time.

**01 Background and aim (0:32–0:50)**
Distal pancreatectomy has moved from open to laparoscopic to robotic surgery. Laparoscopy is limited by dexterity and visualisation, and whether the robot improves outcomes is still debated. So we compared all three approaches.

**02 Why robotic? (0:50–1:03)**
The robot offers 3D vision, tremor filtration, motion scaling and wristed instruments. Almost all evidence comes from the da Vinci platform.

**03 Operative technique (1:03–1:20)** *[Fig. 1, steps 1–5]*
Port placement is shown here, with the five key steps alongside: position and dock, enter the lesser sac, control the splenic vessels, divide the pancreas, and resect en bloc. The spleen can be preserved.

**04 Clinical outcomes (1:20–1:58)** *[Table: green, grey, amber]*
Green favours RDP. Against open surgery, RDP has less blood loss, fewer complications, a shorter stay and more spleen preservation. Against laparoscopy, complication rates are similar, but RDP has fewer conversions, more spleen preservation and better oncological results. The one amber cell is operating time, mainly because of docking.

**05 Cost-effectiveness (1:58–2:16)** *[Three cost tiles]*
Theatre costs are higher. However, the overall cost is similar to laparoscopy and lower than open surgery, because the shorter stay offsets it. RDP also gives more quality-adjusted life years than laparoscopy.

**06 Challenges (2:16–2:28)**
The barriers are docking time, few training centres, and high upfront cost, which leads to unequal access.

**Conclusion (2:28–2:45)** *[Navy conclusion box, QR code]*
To conclude, RDP offers greater precision, better outcomes and more spleen preservation, and it is cost-effective. Wider adoption needs training and equitable access. The QR code links to the full paper. Thank you.

## Likely questions (short answers)
- **Is this a systematic review?** "No, it's a clinical (narrative) review of the published comparative studies, meta-analyses and cost analyses."
- **Where does the extra 30 minutes come from?** "Mostly robot docking and set-up, and it shortens along the learning curve."
- **What about pancreatic fistula?** "Rates are similar between robotic and laparoscopic, and lower than open."
